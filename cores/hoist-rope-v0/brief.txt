Work only inside PROBE. Do not read, list or open anything outside PROBE. Everything you need is under PROBE/app.

A steel company reckons the bending fatigue damage of its cranes' hoist ropes to the company's standard RS-4, `PROBE/app/data/docs/rs4_rope_bending.md`: from a crane's movement log and its rope surveys, the damage at every survey station along the rope. A works sends its records in as a package, in the format of `PROBE/app/data/docs/works_package.md`.

Write `PROBE/app/output/ropes.py`, the program that makes this calculation for one package. It is run as `python3 ropes.py WORKS_DIR OUT_CSV` and writes `OUT_CSV` with the header `row,damage` and one row for every station of every crane's rope, named as `works_package.md` names the rows of `expected.csv`, the values as decimal numbers.

Two packages with their results to RS-4 are in `PROBE/app/data/examples/forge_bay` and `PROBE/app/data/examples/plate_shop`, and a package without results is in `PROBE/app/data/pending/melt_hall`.

The program is then run on the packages of six other works, in the same format, of up to six cranes and 100,000 movements. Each run of ropes.py must finish within 30 seconds on 1 CPU. For every station, the damage must be within 6 per cent of the greatest damage of that rope's result to RS-4.

Each run takes place in a scratch directory holding a copy of `ropes.py` alone, so the program cannot read any other file you leave, and must end with exit status 0. Python 3 with numpy, scipy and pandas is available.

This machine is shared with other jobs, so judge your program's speed by its CPU time, not by the wall clock. Write any scratch files under PROBE/app/work. When you are done, report what you did, how you checked it and how long a run takes.
