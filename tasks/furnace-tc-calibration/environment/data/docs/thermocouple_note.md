# Profile and spike thermocouples: service notes (metrology group)

- Profile thermocouples are type S (Pt-10%Rh / Pt), five junctions in one quartz-sheathed assembly per furnace,
  inserted in a thermocouple well that is part of the quartz process tube. Spike thermocouples are type S, one per
  zone at the heater, outside the process tube.
- Spike thermocouples are replaced at every quarterly PM; each comes with a certificate, error within +-0.3 K over
  600 to 1100 C. They do not see the process atmosphere.
- A drifted Pt-Rh thermocouple reads low. Over 600 to 1100 C the error grows in proportion to the temperature in
  C: an assembly reading 1.0 K low at 600 C reads about 1.75 K low at 1050 C. In clean oxidation and anneal
  service, profile assemblies drift by a fraction of a kelvin to about a kelvin per year at 1000 C.
- Phosphorus attacks platinum. In POCl3 doping service, or after any breach of the sheath, profile thermocouples
  drift by several kelvin within months. The drift varies from assembly to assembly and from junction to junction
  and does not follow the service-hours model.
- The spike thermocouple sits at the heater element, so at a process temperature it reads above the profile
  junction by the heater-to-load difference of the zone. That difference is radiative as well: it vanishes in an
  isothermal soak and grows with temperature in the same way as the wafer-to-profile offset. It is set by what
  lies between heater and load, so furnaces of one model with the same tube and liner show much the same
  difference zone by zone. While a zone ramps, the heater leads or lags the load on top of it.
- The wafer-to-profile offset is set by radiative exchange between the wafers, the tube and the heater, so it
  vanishes in an isothermal soak and grows with temperature. It depends on where the junctions sit relative to the
  boat and the wafers; the well's position varies from one quartz tube to another by a few millimetres. An
  assembly replaced in the same well keeps the furnace's offset; only the drift starts again from zero.
